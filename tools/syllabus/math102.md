# Math 102 syllabus (Term 261)

Textbook: Larson & Edwards, *Calculus: Early Transcendental Functions, Metric Version*, 7th ed.
Course: Calculus II (4-0-4). Prerequisite: Math 101. Exam I = 14 MCQ, Exam II = 14 MCQ, Final = 20 MCQ and comprehensive.

| Sec | Topic |
|---|---|
| 5.2 | Area (sigma notation and summation formulas, upper and lower sums, area as a limit of sums, midpoint rule) |
| 5.3 | Riemann sums and definite integrals (limit definition, properties of definite integrals, evaluating limits of sums as integrals) |
| 5.4 | The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (both parts, Mean Value Theorem for integrals, average value of a function, F(x) = integral from a to g(x) with the second FTC, net change, total distance; Exercise 114 is included) |
| 5.5 | Integration by substitution (change of limits, even and odd functions, symmetry) |
| 5.7 | The natural logarithmic function: integration (log rule, integrals of the six trig functions) |
| 5.8 | Inverse trigonometric functions: integration (including completing the square) |
| 5.9 | Hyperbolic functions (integration only, Theorem 5.21) |
| 7.1 | Area of a region between two curves (integrating with respect to x or y, intersecting curves, regions split into pieces) |
| 7.2 | Volume: the disk method (disks and washers, axis of revolution not a coordinate axis, solids with known cross sections) |
| 7.3 | Volume: the shell method |
| 7.4 | Arc length and surfaces of revolution |
| 8.1 | Basic integration rules |
| 8.2 | Integration by parts (repeated parts, tabular method) |
| 8.3 | Trigonometric integrals (powers of sin, cos, tan, sec, products such as sin(mx)cos(nx)) |
| 8.4 | Trigonometric substitution |
| 8.5 | Partial fractions |
| 8.7 | Rational functions of sine and cosine (page 569 only: the substitution u = tan(x/2)) |
| 8.8 | Improper integrals (infinite limits, infinite discontinuities, convergence and divergence) |
| 9.1 | Sequences (limits, monotonic and bounded sequences, squeeze theorem) |
| 9.2 | Series and convergence (geometric series, telescoping series, nth-term test) |
| 9.3 | The integral test and p-series |
| 9.4 | Comparison of series (direct and limit comparison) |
| 9.5 | Alternating series (alternating series test, remainder estimate, absolute and conditional convergence) |
| 9.6 | The ratio and root tests |
| 9.7 | Taylor polynomials and approximation (up to Example 7) |
| 9.8 | Power series (radius and interval of convergence) |
| 9.9 | Representation of functions by power series |
| 9.10 | Taylor and Maclaurin series; binomial series |

Typical coverage: Exam I = 5.2 to 7.1, Exam II = 7.2 to 8.7, Final = comprehensive (all sections above, with
roughly the second half of the questions on improper integrals and Chapter 9).
In earlier terms (221 to 253) Exam I and Exam II used a similar split: Exam I = Riemann sums, FTC, substitution,
log/inverse trig/hyperbolic integrals and area between curves; Exam II = volumes, arc length, surface area and the
techniques of integration; the Final adds improper integrals, sequences, series and Taylor/Maclaurin series.
Older terms may have covered slightly different sections. Keep those questions and solve them with the
textbook method for that section.

Students must memorize the power series of 1/(1+x), e^x, sin x, cos x, arctan x and (1+x)^k (textbook page 674).

Explicitly omitted or limited in the term 261 syllabus: Section 8.7 is only the page 569 material (tan(x/2)
substitution); Section 9.7 only up to Example 7.
Not in the term 261 schedule (so not examined): 5.1 (taken as prerequisite), 5.6 (indeterminate forms and L'Hopital's rule, covered
in Math 101), 5.9 other than integration of hyperbolic functions, 6 (differential equations), 7.5 to 7.7 (work,
moments, fluid force), 8.6 (numerical integration: trapezoidal and Simpson rules), Chapter 10 and beyond.

Prerequisites (allowed freely): Math 101 differentiation, including derivatives of exponential, logarithmic, inverse
trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, and high-school algebra and trigonometry.
