# Stat 201 syllabus (Term 261)

Source: https://math.kfupm.edu.sa/docs/default-source/css-library/stat201-261.pdf (saved as syl261.pdf in Ice_Math_OldExams_sources/stat201).
Parts marked "STATED" come from that syllabus (or, in the last two sections, from the STAT 319 syllabi named there).
Parts marked "INFERRED" are read off the old exams (STAT 319 terms 222 to 243 and STAT 201 terms 251, 252) and are not in a syllabus.

Textbook (STATED): Montgomery & Runger, *Applied Statistics and Probability for Engineers*, 6th ed. (2014).
Course (STATED): STAT 201, Probability and Statistics for Engineers and Scientists (3-1-3). Prerequisite: MATH 102. Software: R and R Studio (lab).
Exam I = 14 MCQ (70 points), Exam II = 14 MCQ (70 points), Final = 20 MCQ (100 points) and comprehensive. Exam dates TBA in the syllabus.
Description (STATED): presentation and interpretation of data, elementary probability, random variables and probability distributions
(binomial, hypergeometric, Poisson, exponential, Weibull, normal, lognormal), estimation, hypothesis testing for mean, variance and
proportion, simple and multiple linear regression.
The syllabus says exam questions are "similar in style and level of difficulty to the examples and exercises provided in the textbook".

## Coverage per exam (STATED, term 261)

- Exam I: Chapters 6, 2, 3 (descriptive statistics, probability, discrete distributions). 14 MCQ, 70 points.
- Exam II: Chapters 4, 7, 8 (continuous distributions, sampling distribution and CLT, confidence intervals). 14 MCQ, 70 points.
- Final: comprehensive, 20 MCQ, 100 points (so also Chapters 9, 11, 12).

## Explicitly omitted or limited (STATED)

- 2-1: only 2.1.1 to 2.1.3.
- 7-2: only Examples 7.1 and 7.2, single-sample case (no two-sample or difference of means).
- 8-3: two-sided interval only. 12-1: mainly R output. 12-5.1: residual plots only.
- Sections not in the pacing schedule are not taught, for example 3-1, 3-5, 3-7 (geometric), 4-1, 4-5 (continuous uniform), 4-7 (normal
  approximation to binomial/Poisson), 8-1.3/8-1.4, 8-5 to 8-7, 9-2.2 (type II error and sample size), 9-2.4 to 9-2.5, 9-6 onward,
  11-7 (adequacy of the model), 12-5.2, 12-6, Chapters 5 and 10.

## Observations from the old exams (INFERRED, not from the syllabus)

- Format: every file is pure MCQ with five options (a) to (e). Terms 233 to 252 follow the current 14/14/20 pattern. The only
  term 222 exam on the site has 18 MCQ (Exam I, 120 min) and the two term 231 files have 18 MCQ each (the old 90-point exams).
  Exam I and II are 90 minutes, finals 120 minutes.
- In the MASTER-version files the correct option is always (a). Eight files print "(correct)" after it, the others do not mark it.
- Exam I (terms 233 to 252) is about 5 or 6 probability questions (Ch 2), 5 or 6 discrete-distribution questions (Ch 3) and 3 descriptive
  statistics questions (Ch 6: outliers by the 1.5 IQR rule, quartiles, histogram or box plot shape, effect of adding a constant).
- Exam II is about 6 or 7 questions on Ch 4 (pdf/cdf from a given density, normal, exponential with memoryless property, Weibull, lognormal
  probabilities and percentiles), 2 on Ch 7 (CLT, probability about a sample mean) and 5 or 6 on Ch 8 (confidence level, sample size, interval
  length, interval for mean or proportion, in 251/252 also an interval for sigma).
- Finals mix about 2 probability, 1 or 2 descriptive, 1 or 2 distribution, 1 sampling, 1 interval, 5 or 6 hypothesis-testing (z test,
  t test with p-value ranges, proportion test, type I/II error and power, chi-square test on sigma in 251/252) and 6 to 8 regression
  questions (simple regression from summary sums, R/Minitab output tables, multiple regression from output, F test, partial F, R^2,
  adjusted R^2, residual plot).
- Type II error and power (9-2.2) are asked on several finals (242 Q9, 251 Q7, 252 Q9 and Q10) although 9-2.2 is not in the pacing list.
  Treat 9-2.2 as in scope for solving old exam questions.
- Questions often carry a textbook tag such as "(Exercise 8 - 28)" or "(Suggested Problem 4 - 98)" (terms 241, 251, 252) and reuse the
  textbook numbers with changed data. The same textbook problem is reused across terms (for example 4-60 cholesterol, 8-28 Izod impact,
  9-1 hypotheses, 11-1 concrete permeability).
- Several finals (233, 251, 252) use one lead-in for 3 to 6 consecutive questions (same regression data or same R output). Each question
  needs the shared lead-in repeated.
- The exam PDFs do not contain z, t, chi-square or F tables. Many answers use unusual levels (78.87%, 98.36%, 99.52%) so they must be
  computed with a calculator or R (inverse normal, t, chi-square, F). In t, chi-square and F items the options often give a critical
  value or a p-value range ("0.1 < p-value < 0.2") that needs a table or software.

Prerequisites (allowed freely, INFERRED from the course description and exams): algebra, integration of simple densities (powers,
exponentials), the gamma function value Gamma(1/2) and Gamma(1 + 1/beta) for Weibull means, exponentials and logarithms.

## STAT 319 compared with STAT 201 (the old STAT 319 exams are in this course)

Sources: STAT 319 syllabi for terms 243 (saved as syl319-243.pdf), 241, 233, 231 and 222 (read, not saved).

### STATED

- STAT 319 (term 243) and STAT 201 (term 261) have the same title (Probability and Statistics for Engineers and Scientists), 3-1-3 credit
  hours, prerequisite MATH 102, the same textbook and edition (Montgomery & Runger, 6th ed., 2014) and the same chapter and section numbers.
  No renumbering is needed. Same exam split too: Exam I = Ch 6, 2, 3 (14 MCQ), Exam II = Ch 4, 7, 8 (14 MCQ), Final = comprehensive (20 MCQ).
- Term 243 STAT 319 lists no topic that STAT 201 (term 261) leaves out. The term 243 plan is a subset of the term 261 plan: sections 6-1 to 6-4,
  2-1 to 2-7, 3-2, 3-3, 3-4, 3-6, 3-8, 3-9, 4-2, 4-3, 4-4, 4-6, 4-8, 4-10, 4-11, 7-1, 7-2, 8-1 (8-1.1, 8-1.2, 8-1.5), 8-2, 8-4, 9-1, 9-2.1,
  9-2.3, 9-3.1, 9-5.1, 11-1 to 11-6, 11-8, 12-1 to 12-4, 12-5.1. The same plan appears in terms 233 and 241.
- STAT 201 (261) adds these items that STAT 319 (233 to 243) does not list: 8-3 (confidence interval on variance and standard deviation,
  two-sided) and 9-4.1 (tests on the variance). Term 251 (the first STAT 201 term) already lists both. Term 261 also names hypergeometric
  in the course description (STAT 319 has 3-8 in its plan but not in its description) and restricts 12-5.1 to residual plots.
- Older STAT 319 syllabi list more sections than the current plan. Term 222 lists 3-1, 3-5 (discrete uniform), 3-7 (geometric and binomial),
  4-1, 4-5 (continuous uniform), 4-7 (normal approximation to the binomial and Poisson), 11-7 (adequacy of the regression model), 12-5.2
  (influential observations) and 12-6 (aspects of multiple regression modeling). Term 231 lists 3-7 (geometric), 4-5 (continuous uniform)
  and 11-7. Term 222 also put Chapters 2, 3, 4 on Exam 1 and descriptive statistics plus Chapters 7, 8 on Exam 2, with 18/18/24 MCQ
  (Exam 1 = 18 MCQ, Exam 2 = 18 MCQ, Final = 24 MCQ, 90/90/120 points).
- Term 231 STAT 319 Exam I and II were 90-point exams; the term 261 Exam I and II are 70 points (14 MCQ).

### INFERRED (from the old STAT 319 exams)

- STAT 319 topics that are NOT in STAT 201 (term 261) and that appear in old exams: geometric distribution (222 Exam I Q8 "fourth patient is
  the first one due to ...", 231 Exam I Q11 "player continues until defeated"), continuous uniform distribution (222 Exam I Q11), the normal
  approximation to the Poisson (222 Exam I Q14, Poisson with mean 10,000). The textbook sections are 3-7, 4-5 and 4-7. These three
  questions are outside the current course; they can be kept as practice but should be flagged as out of the term 261 scope.
- Other STAT 319 exam items are in STAT 201 scope: everything on 233, 241, 242 and 243 papers stays within the term 261 section list
  (type II error/power and partial F-test excepted, which are asked in STAT 201 too).
- The STAT 319 papers (222 to 243) never ask for a confidence interval or test on sigma. The STAT 201 papers 251 and 252 do
  (251 final Q10 chi-square critical value, 252 Exam 2 Q10 interval for sigma). So the chi-square distribution appears only
  in STAT 201 papers.
- Because the section numbers are identical, no mapping table is needed. A STAT 319 question tagged "(Q: 4-73, page 143)" or
  "Exercise 8-28" refers to the same textbook problem a STAT 201 student would see.
